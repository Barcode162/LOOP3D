# Rubric: The Loft

**Use it for:** milestones 3 and 4, the two-floor room, empty and lit or lived in, at night or at sunset.

**The reference, in words** (`docs/CRAFT.md`, section 1, "The lofts").
A big volume with a small human corner in it: a double-height room, then a low warm place to sit. Light comes
from many small warm sources and none is bare: strips hidden under every step, shelf and ledge (2,700 to
3,000 K), pendants over the counter, lamps by the bed; the ceiling is rarely the source. Outside is cool: a
city in blue at night, or the last orange of the sun against cool shade. A wall of glass the height of the
room, divided by thin dark bars; at night it is a dark mirror that shows the room back, with the city
through it. A stair with a lit rail climbs to a mezzanine bed; a kitchen sits under it; a deep sofa stands on
a shaggy rug. Hundreds of small things stand in groups of three and five with gaps between: books, jars,
plants, frames, cushions, a tray on the table. Soft things are low (rugs, throws, sofas), hard things high
(steel, glass, brick, timber). Only three or four surfaces, repeated: one wood, one metal, one fabric family,
one stone or brick. Something has just happened: a person, a cat, a pan on the hob, a lit screen. Depth in
layers: something close and dark at the frame's edge, the seating in the middle, the view far.

## The ten questions

1. **Light.** Is every pool of light owned by a strip, a pendant or a lamp you can find, are the sources
   themselves hidden or shaded, and are the corners left dark (about a twentieth of the bright spots)?
2. **Surfaces.** At the nearest thing (the sofa's arm, the counter, the floor), do you believe the wood, the
   fabric, the metal? Does the floor repeat the bright things above it, stretched towards you?
3. **Edges and contact.** Rounded edges that catch the strips' light; furniture pressing into the rug;
   cushions that sag where they meet; a dark seam under everything.
4. **Detail in three sizes.** The volume, stair and glass wall; furniture and its parts; the small things on
   shelves and tables.
5. **Depth.** A dark near thing at the frame's edge, the seating, then the glass and the city: three layers,
   each lit differently.
6. **Scale.** Is the room truly double height against a sofa, a step (0.18 m), a door, a person? Is every
   piece of furniture its real size?
7. **Variety and life.** Things in odd groups with gaps, shelves two thirds full, nothing evenly spread; and
   a sign that someone lives here (a person, a cat, a book face-down, a pan on the hob).
8. **Warm pools against a cool outside.** Are there exactly two families of colour, warm inside and cool
   outside, far apart in kelvin, with no third? Does the warm fall off into shadow instead of filling the room?
9. **The room in the glass.** At night, does the glass show the room back as a dim mirror, the lamps
   doubled in it, with the city seen through and its bars thin and dark?
10. **Cleanliness.** No grain in the dark corners, no light leaking under walls, no glow round the strips
    beyond a little bloom, no shimmer on the thin bars.

Questions 8 and 9 stand in place of the base's colour and life; life moved into question 7.
