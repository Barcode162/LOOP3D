# Rubric: Your Hill

**Use it for:** milestone 1, the desert slope from the user's five photos, in any view of it: the ground at
your feet, the slope, the valley, the ranges.

**The reference, in words** (`docs/CRAFT.md`, section 1, "Your hill").
A mid-morning in clear dry air. A high sun, so shadows are small, hard and nearly black. The sky is pale at
the horizon and a very deep blue overhead, with no cloud.
*The ground:* pale decomposed granite and grit, the colour of dry sand with a little pink, between dark,
pitted volcanic stones from the size of a fist to the size of a chair; some lighter angular pieces; rust-red
patches of low dried plants; small yellow flowers in drifts.
*The plants, each keeping its own distance from the next:* cholla, jointed and covered in pale spines that
glow white when the sun is behind them; yucca, a ball of stiff blades on a shaggy trunk of dead leaves;
Joshua trees, forked and tufted; grey skeleton shrubs; green broom-like bushes; small barrel cactus with red
spines.
*The slope:* steeper above, with bigger boulders near the top where the rock breaks out.
*The valley:* flat, evenly dotted with scrub to the horizon, cut by straight pale dirt roads; small low
houses and sheds far apart; a few dark cypress by one house; greener squares where someone waters.
*The distance:* range behind range, each bluer and flatter, the last almost the colour of the sky.

The user has stood there. When the picture and these words disagree, the user's eye decides, and this file
is corrected.

## The ten questions

1. **Light.** One hard sun and the sky, nothing else: are shadows small, sharp and nearly black, and is the
   lit grit bright without burning out?
2. **Surfaces.** At your feet, do you believe the grit and the stone: grains, pits, a dusty dullness, no
   shine?
3. **Edges and contact.** Do stones sit *in* the grit, a little buried, each with its dark seam, and do
   plants come out of the ground rather than stand on it?
4. **Detail in three sizes.** The range and the slope; the gullies, outcrops and boulders; the stones, tufts
   and flowers within two metres. Is each there?
5. **Depth.** A near plant or stone, the slope, the valley, then ranges going paler and bluer: can you count
   at least three ranges by their colour alone?
6. **Scale.** Do the far houses, the roads and the known plants (a yucca is about a person's height) make the
   valley feel kilometres wide?
7. **Variety.** Does each plant keep its elbow room without falling into a grid, with old and young together,
   and does any stone or plant visibly repeat?
8. **The stones against the grit.** Are the stones as dark as in the photos (close to coal on their shaded
   side), pitted, a little varnished on top, and is the grit as pale? This contrast is the place.
9. **The cholla lit from behind.** Looking towards the sun, do the spines glow as a pale halo round a darker
   body, and do the yucca's blades catch a line of light?
10. **Cleanliness.** No shimmer in the far scrub, no seam between near and far land, no popping plants, no
    haze banding in the sky.

Questions 8 and 9 stand in place of the base's colour and life: here the colour is the dark stone on pale
grit under a blue sky, and the life is the plants.
