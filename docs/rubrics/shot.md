# Rubric: a shot

**Use it for:** one shot of a film (its first, middle and last frame side by side, with the beat it serves
written under it), or one frame of a game camera. Judged on an upright phone first.

**The reference, in words** (`docs/CRAFT.md`, sections 9 and 10).
Every shot is for something. Its size says what: extreme wide for where we are, wide for what is happening,
medium for talk and hands, close for feeling, an insert for the thing itself. Its lens says how: 14 to 24 mm
vast, 28 to 35 natural, 50 as the eye sees one thing, 85 to 135 flattering and squeezed. The camera is still
unless it has a reason: a push in means this matters, a pull out means leaving, a rise reveals. The subject
sits on a third, with a little headroom and with room in front of where they look or move. There is
something near, the subject, and something far. On an upright phone the three depths stack vertically, faces
sit in the upper third, and tall things (stairs, towers, falls, trees) do the work. Between two shots of
one thing the angle changes by 30 degrees or more and the size clearly; the camera stays on one side of the
line between two people. A shot lasts as long as it has something new to give.

## The ten questions

1. **Size.** Does the shot's size suit what the beat is for (place, action, talk, feeling, a detail)?
2. **Lens and height.** Does the lens suit it (wide for land and cramped rooms, long for faces), and is the
   camera's height chosen (eye level, low to look up, high to look down), with uprights upright?
3. **The move.** Is it still, or does it move for a reason you can say in three words, starting and ending
   gently?
4. **Composition.** Is the subject on a third, with headroom and lead room, and the horizon off the middle?
5. **Depth.** Something near, the subject, something far; or a frame within the frame (a doorway, a
   shoulder, branches).
6. **Where the eye lands.** Is the subject the brightest, sharpest or most contrasted thing, and do lines
   lead to it?
7. **Focus.** Is the right thing sharp, and does the blur behind suit the shot (soft for a close-up, all
   sharp for land)?
8. **The upright phone.** Does the frame work held upright, with the depths stacked and nothing that
   matters lost off the sides?
9. **The cut.** Against the shots before and after: 30 degrees and a clear change of size, the same side of
   the line, matching eyelines, a length that suits (wide 4 to 8 s, close 2 to 4, action under 2)? For a
   game camera: does the player keep control, is the horizon level, is there room ahead of the way they go?
10. **Cleanliness.** Nothing cut at a joint by the frame's edge, nothing growing out of a head, no jitter,
    no swimming focus, nothing passing through the camera.
