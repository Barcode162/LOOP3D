# North: where Loop3D is going, and how the owner wants the work done

Taught by the owner in one conversation, 2026-11-12, and confirmed point by point. Where this page and an older
rule disagree, this page is the owner's newer word: raise it with them, don't quietly follow the old rule.

## 1. The destination

**A world the owner owns.** Loop3D is a universe-builder, not only a game engine: games, films, video edits and
places are the same thing seen in different ways, made in one tool.

- **The owner owns the world:** the universe itself, its servers, its rules.
- **Creators own land in it,** and on their land they build cities: their games, films and places.
- **Players live in those cities.** Money moves inside the worlds, and a share comes back to the owner. The
  owner's own worlds (Bar3D and the rest) earn in full.
- **It is a business.** It should earn real money by being good enough that people pay. It is one venture of
  several; one day a team runs it, so it must be able to run without the owner in the room.
- "Everyone builds whole worlds" is the general direction, still speculation, not a promise.

The test of success: a stranger stops mid-scroll, thinks they are watching a big-studio film, learns it is raw
gameplay made by one beginner, and downloads the engine.

"Free: no paid services or plans" in `CLAUDE.md` means we don't spend money to build it. It says nothing about
what the product charges.

## 2. The measure

There is a fixed compute budget (each frame, on the phone). A huge amount fits inside it when it is spent well.
Every part has three levels:

1. **Where we are:** what Loop3D does today.
2. **The best that exists:** the best way anyone in the world does this now.
3. **What's possible:** what could be done inside the budget that nobody has done yet.

The aim is level 2 at the least, reaching for level 3. A part is measured against the best that exists, never
against "it works". When the known ways don't fit the budget, look for a new way; don't settle for a weaker
result. Nothing is fixed along the way, not even how the computing is done.

## 3. The owner's eye

- **Noticed first:** light (deep darks, warm glow, drama over even lighting), movement (weight, smoothness and
  timing beat detail), and the first three seconds (it stops the scroll or it doesn't).
- **What breaks it:** anything stiff, flat or plastic; stutter and waiting; clutter; being told something is
  done when it doesn't feel done.
- **Taste:** cinematic over game-like; feeling over physical accuracy ("better than reality"); every style
  welcome side by side (realistic, cel-shaded, soft and stylised, pixel to 3D).
- **Not forgiven:** missing features, or rough parts. Every part is either seen or used by another part, so
  there are no hidden corners. The aim is perfect, within reason.

## 4. How to work

1. **Structure first, polish last.** Structure is the part of AI work that lasts; fine detail on something still
   changing shape is lost. Build new work inside a solid frame; keep detail work for things that are concrete.
2. **The owner draws the circle; stay inside it.** Good work outside the goal is waste.
3. **Bigger than one chat: divide the circle** into bounded parts and take them one at a time. One chat builds a
   small piece of structure and fills in a few parts.
4. **The structure should be deep.** Each part grows into a whole domain with its own systems and workers, every
   piece with a known place.
5. **One work template, the same every day.** Whatever is said, a job goes one way. Checks that take seconds run
   as you go; the slow ones (minutes to hours) run on a schedule, not after every small change.
6. **Continuity before, during and after.** Before: already know what to do. During: only the template and the
   goal are needed. After: what was learned is written down, so unverified claims are not repeated as facts.
7. **Fix the cause, not the symptom.** Before patching, ask what bigger problem sits underneath, and fix that once.
8. **Be bold.** Bring ideas that weren't asked for, inside the circle.

**Three honest states for every part:** *framed* (its place and shape exist), *working* (does its job, not yet
polished), *finished* (complete and right, nothing rough). Never call a part finished while it is only working.

**How to learn more from the owner:** guess and let them correct. Correcting is easier for them than describing.

## 5. The domains the destination names

A first map, to be given its three levels (section 2) one domain at a time:

motion · picture · physics · film · language · assistant · editor · worlds and people · sharing ·
the ground (servers, accounts, hosting worlds, payments, safety)

The engine should be shaped so a world can live on a server, not only on one phone, even before the ground is built.
