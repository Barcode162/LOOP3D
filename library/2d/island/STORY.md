# Tidehollow — the story, the lore, and the whole truth

A survival island farming and fishing game, Stardew Valley style — but the
island is haunted by a bargain it made with the sea, and the only way to
understand what happened here is to talk to everyone, do their quests, and put
the pieces together. No single character knows the whole story. The player
does, by the end — if they earn it.

Tone: dramatic, spooky, fun, mystical. Cozy by day, wrong by night.

## The island

**Tidehollow** is a small island that does not appear on any map twice. Sailors
find it only when the fog wants them to. It has a farmhouse, a dock, a
lighthouse, a well, a market stall, a church with no priest, and a festival
field where nothing has been celebrated for sixty years.

The island feeds anyone who works it — fish always bite, crops always grow,
the soil is black and warm even in winter. That is the first thing every
newcomer notices. The second thing, if they stay, is the price.

### The rules of Tidehollow

Every child on the island learns these before they learn to swim:

1. Never answer when the bell rings your name.
2. Never eat a white fish.
3. Don't follow lights on the water.
4. If the scarecrow is facing the sea, stay inside.
5. The well knows your name. Don't tell it anything else.
6. Count the boats in the morning. If there is one more than yesterday, don't board it.

### The Bargain

In the first year of the settlement, the founder Amos — the first farmer —
went down to the shore at low tide and spoke to the water. The thing that
answered called itself the Hollow Tide, or the Mother Below: the sea that
remembers everything it has ever swallowed.

The deal: the island would feed its people forever — full nets, full fields,
no hunger, no rot. In exchange, every seven years, the tide would take its
Tithe: one person. Not killed. Taken. Some walk into the sea smiling. Some
forget who they are and wander off on a mainland boat. Some are simply not
there in the morning, their bed still warm.

The village calls it the Calling. Nobody says the word "sacrifice" out loud.

### The Drowned Bell

Out in the bay, under the water, there is a bell from a ship that sank before
the lighthouse was built. On moonless nights it rings — once for every year
since the Bargain, and then once more, for the person it wants next. If you
hear your own name inside the ringing, the tide has chosen you.

### The Pale Tide

Once a year, in late autumn, the tide comes in white and still, like milk.
The fish float belly-up and whisper the names of the Tithed. The white fish
are the ones that remember too much. That is why you never eat them.

### The lighthouse

Built by Elias Vane, the first keeper. Its light still burns every night and
no one alive tends it. The last keeper, Tomas, vanished forty years ago. His
wife still sets a place for him at the table.

## The characters — when they came, why, and what they hide

### Elder June — born here, 78. The witness.

June is the oldest person on the island and the only one who remembers the
Night of Empty Tables, because she was nine and hiding under a table when it
happened. She keeps the true history in a locked chest under her bed: a
festival photograph with forty faces, and a list of every Tithe, in her
mother's handwriting.

- **What she hides:** she knows the Bargain's exact words, and she knows the
  Tithe is due this year. She has been waiting for someone to arrive who is
  not afraid of the truth.
- **Quest chain — "The Girl Under the Table":** (1) bring her the three
  things the tide took from her family — her brother's name, carved on
  driftwood; the song her mother sang, which only the well remembers; the
  festival photograph, which the crab stole. (2) Help her set forty places
  at the festival field, one for each of the vanished. (3) Sit with her on
  the longest night and listen to the whole story.
- **What she reveals:** the Bargain, the Night of Empty Tables, and the fact
  that the Tithe can be refused — but only by someone the island itself
  chose. She gives the player the first page of the truth.

### Sailor Bram — arrived Year -30. The shipwreck.

Bram's ship hit the rocks in fog that "came out of a clear sky". He has
repaired the *Merry Widow* eleven times. Every time, the hull rots overnight,
exactly where the tide touches it. He stays because the island won't let his
ship leave — and because the sea owes him an answer.

- **What he hides:** his ship's log, salvaged from the reef, says the fog
  that wrecked him was *called* — something sang the ship onto the rocks.
  The same song the bell rings.
- **Quest chain — "The Merry Widow":** (1) dive the reef at low tide and
  salvage the ship's log. (2) Repair the hull with wood that has never
  touched seawater — pine from the old festival field. (3) Sail out with him
  at dawn and see what the fog does when it meets a ship that refuses it.
- **What he reveals:** the tide is not mindless — it chooses, it sings, it
  collects. He gives the player the second page: the Bargain is a *deal*,
  and deals have loopholes.

### Fisher Finn — arrived Year -12. The brother.

Finn came looking for his brother Ebb, who came to the island for work a year
earlier. Finn arrived too late: the last Tithe took Ebb. Finn has fished the
bay every day since, because of the story the old sailors tell — the Kingfish,
a great pale fish, carries the memories of everyone the tide has taken. Catch
it, and you can ask it one question.

- **What he hides:** he has hooked the Kingfish three times. Each time he
  cut the line. He is terrified of the answer.
- **Quest chain — "The Kingfish":** (1) catch the five whisper-fish that
  swim with the Kingfish. (2) Fish the bay on the night of the Pale Tide,
  when the Kingfish surfaces. (3) Choose: ask it where Ebb is — or ask it
  how to break the Bargain.
- **What he reveals:** the Tithed are not dead. They are *kept* — in the
  tide caves, in the water, in the fish. He gives the player the third page:
  the Tithe can be undone, if someone is willing to pay a different price.

### Shopkeeper Mia — arrived Year -8. The ledger.

Mia inherited the shop from her aunt, who got on a boat one morning and never
got off it — the boat was found drifting, empty, her shopping list still in
her hand. The tide took her mind and left her body wandering somewhere on the
mainland. Mia keeps the shop's ledger, and the ledger is more than accounts:
every debt the island owes is written in it, in her aunt's hand, in a column
headed *owed to the sea*.

- **What she hides:** the last page of the ledger is missing. Her aunt tore
  it out the night before she left. Mia has been looking for it for eight
  years. It is in the lighthouse.
- **Quest chain — "The Missing Page":** (1) balance the shop's books — every
  customer, every debt, including the ones owed to the sea. (2) Find out who
  has been buying bread with coins from a ship that sank in 1887. (3) Climb
  the lighthouse and take back the missing page.
- **What she reveals:** the exact terms of the Bargain, written as a bill:
  *plenty, in exchange for one soul every seven years, until the debt is
  paid.* And the debt is almost paid. One more Tithe — or one willing gift —
  ends it. She gives the player the fourth page.

### Blacksmith Ash — arrived Year -5. The bell-maker.

Ash was exiled from the mainland for forging a bell that would not ring — a
bell for a church that had lost its voice. He came to Tidehollow because he
heard, in a dream, a bell ringing underwater. He knows what the Drowned Bell
is: it is not a warning. It is a *summons*. And a summons can be answered.

- **What he hides:** he has been forging its twin for five years — the
  Counter-Bell, a bell tuned to answer the Drowned Bell and talk back. It is
  almost finished. It needs three things: iron from the reef, coal that has
  never been wet, and a memory, freely given, as fuel.
- **Quest chain — "The Counter-Bell":** (1) dive the reef for the bell-metal
  of the wreck. (2) Bring him coal from the mainland — Sol's cart has some,
  but Sol wants something in trade. (3) Give the bell a memory: any memory,
  freely given. The bell will remember it forever, and the player will not.
- **What he reveals:** the Drowned Bell can be rung *back*. That is the key
  to the whole story — the tide can be spoken to, not just obeyed. He gives
  the player the fifth page.

### Baker Rose — arrived Year -3. The widow.

Rose's bread never stales, because she bakes with tidewater. Anyone who eats
it dreams of the sea that night — calm water, a voice calling a name they
almost know. She came to the island looking for her husband Tomas, the
lighthouse keeper who vanished forty years ago. She still sets a place for
him at the table every night.

- **What she hides:** Tomas is not dead. She has seen him — in the lighthouse
  window, on nights when the light burns blue instead of gold. He is keeping
  the light now, from the other side of it. He cannot leave, and he cannot
  speak, except through dreams.
- **Quest chain — "The Loaf of Returning":** (1) bake with her using
  tidewater, and dream the dream, and remember it. (2) Find Tomas's
  keeper's log in the lighthouse — the last entry is a recipe. (3) Bake the
  Loaf of Returning on the night of the Pale Tide, and leave it on the
  lighthouse step.
- **What she reveals:** the keepers of the light are the Tithed who chose to
  stay — the island keeps its own. Tomas chose to tend the light rather than
  be taken. There is always a choice, even at the end. She gives the player
  the sixth page.

### Merchant Sol — arrived Year -1. The one who can't leave.

Sol is a travelling merchant who came to sell things and cannot leave. His
cart wheel broke on the dock road, and every replacement rots overnight. He
trades in memories and rumours, and his stories about the island are all
wrong in exactly the way a mainlander's stories would be wrong. He is funny,
he is a coward, and he is the only person on the island who has never believed
in any of it — which makes him the only one the tide has never noticed.

- **What he hides:** his "worthless" cargo — the crate he can't sell — is
  full of the Tithed people's belongings, bought cheap on the mainland from
  a boat that drifted in with no crew. He has been carrying them for years
  without knowing what they are.
- **Quest chain — "The Worthless Crate":** (1) help him sell the unsellable
  — a left shoe, a broken compass, a wedding ring, a child's drawing of the
  sea. (2) Return each item to its owner on the island — or to their family.
  (3) Open the last box in the crate. It has the player's name on it. It has
  always had the player's name on it.
- **What he reveals:** the mainland knows the island exists, and the mainland
  has been *buying* what the tide leaves behind. The bargain leaks. And the
  crate with the player's name means the island was expecting the player.
  He gives the player the seventh page.

### Kid Pip — born here, 9. The one who talks to the scarecrow.

Pip is the only child on the island. He talks to the scarecrow in the
festival field, and the scarecrow talks back — or so Pip says. Adults smile
and say nothing. Pip knows where the Tithed go: the tide caves, under the
north cliff, where the water glows blue and the fish swim in slow circles.
He has been there. He is not afraid. Children are braver than adults about
the things adults have agreed not to see.

- **What he hides:** nothing. That is the point. Pip tells the truth because
  nobody has ever told him not to.
- **Quest chain — "The Scarecrow's Friend":** (1) bring the scarecrow a hat
  — it likes hats. (2) Follow Pip to the tide caves at low tide. (3) Play
  the game the Tithed children play: count the boats in the morning, and
  tell Pip which one wasn't there yesterday.
- **What he reveals:** the way into the tide caves, where the Tithed are
  kept — and the fact that they are not suffering. They are waiting. He
  gives the player the eighth page: the door, and how to open it.

### The player — the farmer. Arrived Year 0. The Ninth.

The player gets a letter with no return address: the farmhouse on Tidehollow
is theirs, left by a grandparent they never met. The player is the grandchild
of Amos, the first farmer — the one who made the Bargain. The island has been
waiting for Amos's blood to come home, because the Bargain was made with a
family, not a village, and only the family can end it.

On the first night, the well whispers the player's name. On the first moonless
night, the bell rings — and rings the player's name last.

The player is the Ninth. Eight truths, eight villagers, eight pages — and the
player is the one who puts them together.

## The main quest — five acts, one year

The story runs across the four seasons of the player's first year. Each act
needs the NPC quest chains around it — the main quest cannot be finished
without the villagers' truths.

### Act 1 — Arrival (spring)

Fix up the farmhouse, plant the first crops, catch the first fish, meet
everyone. The well whispers the player's name on the first night. Pip says
the scarecrow is facing the sea. The player finds the first of Amos's letters
hidden in the farmhouse wall: *"If you are reading this, the island chose
you. It chooses one of us, once a generation. Don't be afraid of the bell."*

### Act 2 — The bell rings (summer)

The first moonless night. The Drowned Bell rings, and the player's name is
inside the ringing. The scarecrow has moved. Bram's log and Mia's ledger
begin to make sense. The player learns the word "Tithe" — and that it is due
this year. The festival field's pumpkins grow overnight, each carved with a
face nobody carved.

### Act 3 — The ledger and the log (autumn)

Mia's missing page, Bram's ship's log, Finn's Kingfish. The three documents
agree: the Bargain is almost paid. One more Tithe — or one willing gift —
ends the debt forever. Ash's Counter-Bell is nearly finished. Rose dreams of
Tomas, and this time he speaks: *"Bring the bell to the light."*

### Act 4 — The Pale Tide (late autumn)

The tide comes in white and still. The fish float belly-up and whisper the
names of the Tithed. The player fishes the Kingfish with Finn, bakes the Loaf
of Returning with Rose, and gives Ash a memory for the Counter-Bell — any
memory, freely given, gone forever. Pip leads the player to the tide caves.
Inside: the Tithed, waiting, patient, not afraid. Ebb is there. He remembers
Finn. He asks the player to tell Finn he is not angry.

### Act 5 — The choice (winter, the longest night)

Midnight, at the lighthouse, with the Counter-Bell. The Drowned Bell rings
from the bay. The player rings back. The Hollow Tide rises up out of the
water — not a monster, but a tired old thing, a sea that has been remembering
for too long — and asks what the player has brought it.

**Three endings, and the player earns the one they choose:**

- **Break the Bell (the brave ending).** Ring the Counter-Bell until the
  Drowned Bell cracks. The Bargain breaks. The Tithed walk out of the sea at
  dawn — Ebb, the miller's wife, the forty from the feast — exactly as they
  were when they were taken. But the plenty fades: the fish grow shy, the
  soil turns ordinary, the lighthouse goes dark. The fog lifts, and
  Tidehollow appears on maps for the first time in a hundred years. The
  island becomes an ordinary place, and that is the miracle.
- **Keep the Bargain (the dark ending).** Renew the deal. The player becomes
  the new Keeper of the light. The island stays magical — full nets, full
  fields, the light burning gold every night. But the player must choose the
  next Tithe in seven years. Or offer their own name. The last shot is the
  player at the lighthouse window, watching a boat arrive in the fog,
  holding a letter with no return address.
- **Give Your Name (the secret ending).** Offer the tide the player's own
  name — every memory of the mainland, freely given. The tide accepts a
  willing gift: the debt is paid, the Tithed return, *and* the plenty stays.
  But no one on the island remembers the player's old life — and neither
  does the player. The farmer belongs to Tidehollow now. The final scene is
  Pip introducing the player to the scarecrow, as if for the first time.

## How the story is told — the rule for the game

- **No single character knows the whole story.** June knows the Bargain.
  Bram knows the tide chooses. Finn knows the Tithed are kept. Mia knows the
  debt is almost paid. Ash knows the bell can be answered. Rose knows the
  keepers chose to stay. Sol knows the island was expecting the player. Pip
  knows the way in. The player is the only one who hears all eight.
- **Quests are the story.** Each NPC has a 3-quest chain. Finishing a chain
  earns a page of the truth — a letter, a log entry, a photograph, a recipe,
  a song. All eight pages, plus Amos's letters in the farmhouse, are needed
  to understand the Bargain before the final night.
- **The farm and the fishing are the story too.** Crops grow best near the
  old festival field, because the field remembers the feast. The Kingfish
  only bites on the night of the Pale Tide. The white fish whisper names.
  The crab steals the player's first fish every time — it is collecting, the
  way the tide collects.
- **The spooky is never explained away.** The lighthouse light still burns
  with no keeper. The well knows the player's name. The scarecrow moves.
  Some things on Tidehollow are simply true, and the game never tells the
  player which ones are magic and which ones are just the island.
